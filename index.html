2,317 Donors & The Long Arc of Alumni Giving (2010–2015)

Six years of gifts to one university, told one chart at a time.

Scroll to begin

A total of $24.7M was raised across 3,913 gifts between 2010 and 2015, growing 45% over the period.

Scholarship funding consistently led allocation, holding 41–45% of total giving every year.

The totals swung hard. Giving fell 21% in 2012, then jumped 59% to $5.05M in 2013. In both swings all three allocations moved together, so the mix hardly changed.

Endowment held second place at 34–39%, and Campus Resource took the remaining 16–22%.

Giving is broad-based rather than concentrated. It takes 1,168 of 2,317 donors, roughly half the base, to account for 80% of all dollars raised.

The Pareto rule says about 20% of donors usually bring in 80% of the money. Here the top 20% (463 donors) gave just 49%.

The biggest givers spread across the first 80% of the ring, while the other 1,149 donors crowd into the last fifth.

Reach extends across all 12 colleges, with Natural Science and Arts & Sciences drawing the widest donor base.

Together those two hold 40% of all donors (932 of 2,317), and the top four colleges hold 70%.

A bigger base doesn’t mean bigger gifts, though. In every college the average donor gave between $8.5K (Communication Arts and Sciences) and $12.5K (Education).

Giving itself flows through a mix of named funds and college-designated scholarships alike: 25 destinations in all.

Endowment is the most concentrated. Just four funds hold 37% of every dollar, led by the Diversity Fund at $2.66M, the single largest destination.

Scholarship is the most spread out, split across 12 colleges and two named funds. Campus Resource is the most even: its seven programs each drew between $594K and $793K.

About this data

This story uses the Tableau Public sample dataset “University Advancement, Donations, and Giving.” The data is fictitious and made for training purposes: no real donors, gifts or university are represented.

It covers 3,913 gift records from 2010 to 2015. Dollar figures in the text are rounded, and every number was checked against the raw data.

One counting note: 698 donors gave to more than one allocation, so donor counts split by allocation add up to more than 2,317.

Data: Tableau Public sample dataset “University Advancement, Donations, and Giving” (fictitious). Created by Urmimala Mandal.
